# **1\. Purpose**

The purpose of this SOP is to define how Blinto converts completed Shopify app research and testing into a clear, evidence-based, SEO-focused content brief.

The content brief must give the writer everything required to produce a useful and trustworthy article without repeating the app research or guessing what should be included.

The process ensures that every Shopify app review:

* Satisfies a clear search intent.  
* Targets relevant keywords naturally.  
* Answers real merchant questions.  
* Uses findings from hands-on testing.  
* Covers important Shopify app entities and concepts.  
* Provides more value than existing competing content.  
* Connects with Blinto’s Shopify services.  
* Supports the wider Shopify app content cluster.  
* Includes clear writing, evidence, visual, and quality requirements.

This SOP must be completed before writing begins.

---

# **2\. SOP Objective**

This SOP helps Blinto:

* Define the correct search intent for each article.  
* Identify primary and secondary keywords.  
* Analyze current competing content.  
* Find meaningful content gaps.  
* Build a differentiated review angle.  
* Organize research into a logical outline.  
* Maintain semantic and topical relevance.  
* Map research evidence to the correct sections.  
* Plan internal links and calls to action.  
* Give writers clear instructions.  
* Reduce unnecessary revisions.  
* Maintain consistency across all Shopify app review content.  
* Build connected content clusters instead of isolated articles.

# **9\. Complete Content Research and Brief Creation Workflow**

## **Step 1: Review the Approved Research Handoff**

The SEO Strategist must first review the complete output from SOP 2\.

### **Review:**

* App or category name  
* Content format  
* Testing level  
* Core merchant problem  
* Best-fit merchant  
* Poor-fit merchant  
* Main features  
* Important limitations  
* Pricing observations  
* Installation experience  
* Onboarding findings  
* Merchant-feedback patterns  
* Integrations  
* Support findings  
* Technical notes  
* App score  
* Differentiators  
* Relevant alternatives  
* Screenshots and evidence  
* Claims that must not be made  
* Required disclosures

### **Questions to Answer**

* Is the research complete enough to support the planned content?  
* Are any important facts missing?  
* Does the evidence support a strong verdict?  
* Are there any technical or legal wording limitations?  
* Does the app-testing level affect how the content must be written?  
* Is the original topic still appropriate after testing?

### **Possible Decisions**

| Decision | Action |
| ----- | ----- |
| Research is sufficient | Continue with the brief |
| Minor information missing | Request a small research update |
| Important information missing | Return task to SOP 2 |
| App findings changed the topic | Revise the content format or angle |
| App cannot support useful content | Escalate for replacement or cancellation |

### **Output**

A confirmed research base for the content brief.

## **Step 2: Confirm the Content Format**

Confirm whether the article will be:

* Category or bulk review  
* Single app review  
* App comparison article

The format approved in SOP 1 may be changed if hands-on research reveals that another format would better serve the user.

### **Example**

A planned single app review may become a comparison article if:

* The app has very low branded search demand.  
* Merchants primarily compare it with another app.  
* The research does not support enough depth for a standalone article.  
* A direct competitor provides a stronger decision-making context.

Any format change must be approved and recorded in ClickUp.

## **Step 4: Define the Merchant Problem**

Every content brief must identify the merchant problem behind the search.

Examples:

* The merchant does not know which app is best for a particular use case.  
* The merchant wants to know whether one app is worth the cost.  
* The merchant is comparing two similar apps.  
* The merchant is concerned about setup difficulty.  
* The merchant needs an app that works with an existing technology stack.  
* The merchant is trying to avoid performance or compatibility problems.  
* The merchant needs a solution that can scale with store growth.

### **Required Statement**

Complete this sentence:

**The reader needs this content because they are trying to decide whether \[app or category\] can help them \[specific merchant goal\] without creating \[main concern or limitation\].**

This statement should guide the entire brief.

## **Step 6: Conduct Keyword Research**

The SEO Strategist must research keywords that match the approved topic and search intent.

## **Keyword Types**

### **Primary Keyword**

The main query the article should satisfy.

Examples:

* Best Shopify review apps  
* Judge.me review  
* Judge.me vs Loox  
* Best Shopify apps for dropshipping

### **Secondary Keywords**

Closely related queries that support the main topic.

Examples:

* Judge.me pricing  
* Judge.me features  
* Is Judge.me free?  
* Judge.me alternatives  
* Judge.me Shopify review  
* Judge.me pros and cons

### **Semantic Keywords and Concepts**

Related terms that help explain the subject naturally.

Examples:

* Product reviews  
* User-generated content  
* Photo reviews  
* Review widgets  
* Review requests  
* Google Shopping reviews  
* Review moderation  
* Shopify integrations  
* Review imports  
* Store conversion

### **Long-Tail Keywords**

Specific questions or use cases.

Examples:

* Is Judge.me good for small Shopify stores?  
* Does Judge.me slow down Shopify stores?  
* Can Judge.me import reviews?  
* Judge.me vs Loox for photo reviews  
* Best Shopify review app for beginners

---

## **Keyword Evaluation Criteria**

Evaluate:

* Search intent  
* Relevance  
* Search demand  
* Ranking difficulty  
* Existing website authority  
* Search-result quality  
* Commercial relevance  
* Long-tail opportunity  
* Connection to Blinto’s services  
* Ability to answer the query with available evidence

### **Important Rule**

Do not choose a keyword only because it has higher search volume.

The primary keyword must accurately match the article’s content and user intent.

## **Step 9: Select Content Competitors**

Select approximately:

* 3–5 direct competitors for a single app review  
* 4–6 competitors for a category review  
* 3–5 competitors for a comparison article

Choose pages that:

* Target the same or highly similar intent.  
* Currently rank or appear highly relevant.  
* Represent different content approaches.  
* Include at least one strong independent source where possible.  
* Include the official app or product source where useful.

Do not select competitors only because they have long content.

| Heading structure | How the content is organized |

Competitor analysis should help Blinto create better content. It should not be used to copy headings, language, opinions, or conclusions.

## **Step 11: Identify Content Gaps**

Content gaps should be based on merchant usefulness, not only missing keywords.

### **Possible Gaps**

* No hands-on testing  
* No clear testing methodology  
* Outdated pricing  
* Missing app limitations  
* Weak merchant-fit explanation  
* No poor-fit merchant guidance  
* No technical validation  
* No setup screenshots  
* No onboarding evaluation  
* No direct feature testing  
* No performance discussion  
* No integration details  
* No support evaluation  
* No recent merchant-feedback patterns  
* No meaningful alternatives  
* Weak final verdict  
* Generic pros and cons  
* No comparison by use case  
* No explanation of pricing at scale  
* No disclosure of testing limitations  
* No distinction between public claims and observed findings

### **Gap Prioritization**

Prioritize gaps that:

* Help merchants make better decisions.  
* Are supported by Blinto’s research.  
* Demonstrate real expertise.  
* Differentiate Blinto from generic affiliate content.  
* Connect with Blinto’s Shopify services.

## **Step 12: Define the Content Angle**

The content angle is the main perspective that makes the article useful and different.

### **Strong Content Angles**

* Best option for a specific merchant type  
* Practical review based on setup and testing  
* Honest evaluation of value at different growth stages  
* Comparison based on real merchant workflows  
* Review focused on ease of use for beginners  
* Review focused on scalability for growing stores  
* Comparison focused on pricing versus feature depth  
* Evaluation based on app-stack compatibility  
* Review focused on implementation and maintenance requirements

### **Required Content Angle Statement**

Complete:

**This content will help \[target audience\] decide \[specific decision\] by evaluating \[main criteria\] using \[testing, research, or comparison evidence\].**

### **Example**

**This content will help small and growing Shopify merchants decide whether Judge.me is the right product review app by evaluating its installation, pricing, customization, review-collection features, and limitations through hands-on testing and recent merchant feedback.**

# **10\. Content Structure Requirements**

## **A. Category or Bulk Review Brief**

### **Recommended H1**

**Best Shopify \[Category\] Apps for \[Merchant Use Case or Year, if relevant\]**

### **Required Sections**

1. Introduction  
2. Quick recommendation summary  
3. Comparison table  
4. How Blinto selected and evaluated the apps  
5. Best app by use case  
6. Individual app evaluations  
7. Pricing comparison  
8. Feature comparison  
9. How to choose the right app  
10. Implementation considerations  
11. Final recommendation  
12. FAQ  
13. Blinto CTA

### **Each App Section Should Include**

* What the app does  
* Best for  
* Main strengths  
* Important limitation  
* Pricing summary  
* Testing level  
* Why it was included  
* Brief verdict

### **Category Article Rule**

Do not give all apps equal positioning.

The brief must clearly identify:

* Best overall  
* Best for beginners  
* Best value  
* Best for advanced stores  
* Best for a specific merchant type  
* Best free option, where appropriate

Recommendations must be supported by evidence.

---

## **B. Single App Review Brief**

### **Recommended H1**

**\[App Name\] Review: Is It Right for Your Shopify Store?**

### **Required Sections**

1. Introduction  
2. Quick verdict  
3. Review methodology and testing disclosure  
4. What is \[App Name\]?  
5. Who is it best for?  
6. Key features  
7. Installation and onboarding  
8. Ease of use  
9. Pricing and value  
10. Integrations  
11. Merchant-feedback patterns  
12. Pros and cons  
13. Important limitations  
14. Alternatives  
15. Final verdict  
16. FAQ  
17. Blinto CTA

### **Single Review Rule**

The outline must provide enough depth to justify a standalone article.

Do not create a separate heading for every small feature.

Group related features based on merchant use cases.

---

## **C. Comparison Article Brief**

### **Recommended H1**

**\[App A\] vs \[App B\]: Which Shopify App Is Better?**

### **Required Sections**

1. Introduction  
2. Quick verdict  
3. Side-by-side comparison table  
4. Who each app is best for  
5. Feature comparison  
6. Installation and onboarding  
7. Ease of use  
8. Pricing and value  
9. Customization  
10. Integrations  
11. Support and documentation  
12. Scalability  
13. Pros and cons of each  
14. Winner by use case  
15. Final recommendation  
16. FAQ  
17. Blinto CTA

### **Comparison Rule**

Use the same evaluation criteria for both apps.

The final verdict should identify:

* When App A is better  
* When App B is better  
* Which app is best for specific merchant types  
* Whether there is a clear overall winner

Avoid forcing a universal winner when both apps serve different users.

**Purpose:**

# **12\. Define the Direct Answer Strategy**

Important questions should receive clear answers early.

Examples:

* Is the app worth using?  
* Who is it best for?  
* How much does it cost?  
* Which app is better?  
* Does the app require coding?  
* What is the best option in the category?

### **Direct Answer Rules**

* Answer the main query in the introduction or quick verdict.  
* Avoid delaying the verdict until the conclusion.  
* Add context after the direct answer.  
* Use clear wording.  
* Do not make stronger claims than the evidence supports.  
* Mention important limitations near the recommendation.

---

# **13\. Plan the Introduction**

The brief should tell the writer how to open the article.

### **Introduction Requirements**

The introduction should:

* Identify the merchant problem.  
* State what the app or category does.  
* Explain what was researched or tested.  
* Preview the decision the article will help with.  
* Avoid a long general explanation of Shopify.  
* Avoid unsupported statistics.  
* Avoid generic ecommerce introductions.

### **Suggested Introduction Flow**

1. Merchant problem  
2. App or category relevance  
3. Blinto’s testing or research approach  
4. Clear article promise

---

# **14\. Plan the Quick Verdict**

Every app review and comparison article should include a concise verdict near the beginning.

### **Quick Verdict Requirements**

Include:

* Overall recommendation  
* Best-fit merchant  
* Main strength  
* Main limitation  
* Testing level where relevant

### **Example Format**

**Quick verdict:**  
\[App\] is a strong option for \[merchant type\] that needs \[main use case\]. Its strongest advantage is \[strength\], but merchants that require \[missing need\] may prefer an alternative.

The verdict must reflect the completed app research.

---

### **Possible Table Columns**

For category reviews:

* App  
* Best for  
* Starting price  
* Free plan  
* Main strength  
* Main limitation  
* Overall score

For comparisons:

* Criteria  
* App A

# **16\. Plan Pros and Cons**

Pros and cons must come from evidence.

### **Good Pros and Cons**

* Specific  
* Merchant-focused  
* Supported by testing  
* Relevant to purchase or installation decisions  
* Balanced

### **Weak Pros and Cons**

* “Easy to use”  
* “Great features”  
* “Good support”  
* “Can be expensive”

These should be made more specific.

### **Better Examples**

* Guided onboarding made the first review-widget setup possible without code.  
* Advanced customization requires the paid plan.  
* Pricing remains predictable for small stores but may offer less value for merchants that only need basic review collection.

---

# **17\. Plan the Final Verdict**

The brief should define what the conclusion must decide.

### **Final Verdict Must Include**

* Who should choose the app  
* Who should avoid it  
* Main reason to select it  
* Main limitation  
* Best alternative where relevant  
* Clear final recommendation  
* No new information

The conclusion should not simply repeat the introduction.

# **21\. Define Trust and Experience Signals**

Every brief must tell the writer how to demonstrate credibility.

## **Required Trust Signals**

Where relevant:

* Explain Blinto’s testing methodology.  
* State the testing level.  
* Mention when the app was tested.  
* Record when pricing was verified.  
* Use original screenshots.  
* Separate tested findings from merchant-reported experiences.  
* Mention technical validation where relevant.  
* Disclose untested features.  
* Include an author or reviewer with relevant Shopify expertise.  
* Include a last-updated date.  
* Disclose sponsorships, partnerships, or affiliate relationships.

### **Important Rule**

Do not describe the content as independent if a commercial relationship affected the review process.

Editorial control must remain with Blinto.

# **22\. Define Writing Style and Tone**

The content should be:

* Practical  
* Honest  
* Merchant-focused  
* Clear  
* Confident but not exaggerated  
* Evidence-based  
* Balanced  
* Easy to scan  
* Helpful to both technical and non-technical readers

### **Writing Rules**

The writer should:

* Use active voice.  
* Use short paragraphs.  
* Explain technical terms.  
* Give direct answers.  
* Use examples where helpful.  
* Explain why features matter.  
* Connect observations to merchant outcomes.  
* Mention limitations clearly.  
* Avoid keyword stuffing.  
* Avoid generic promotional claims.  
* Avoid robotic transitions.  
* Avoid repeating the same point in several sections.  
* Avoid copying app marketing language.

---

# **23\. Define Claims and Wording Restrictions**

The brief must identify any claims that require caution.

## **Claims Requiring Strong Evidence**

* The app increases sales.  
* The app improves conversion rates.  
* The app is the best in the category.  
* The app does not affect store speed.  
* The app is completely secure.  
* The app works with every theme.  
* The app has excellent support.  
* The app is suitable for every Shopify store.  
* The app is cheaper than all competitors.

### **Safer Wording**

Instead of:

**This app will increase conversions.**

Use:

**The app provides features designed to support conversion-focused offers, but results will depend on the store, offer strategy, and implementation.**

The brief should list any unsupported claims that the writer must avoid.

# **31\. Common Mistakes to Avoid**

Do not:

* Start the brief before app research is approved.  
* Choose the primary keyword only by search volume.  
* Copy a competitor’s outline.  
* Create headings only to insert keywords.  
* Ignore the merchant’s actual decision.  
* Turn every feature into a separate section.  
* Include entities that do not improve the content.  
* Create a long FAQ with repetitive questions.  
* Use unverified app claims.  
* Ignore hands-on research findings.  
* Hide negative findings.  
* Recommend an app without defining who it suits.  
* Use the same content angle for every app.  
* Force a universal winner in comparison content.  
* Add irrelevant internal links.  
* Use an aggressive service CTA.  
* Include exact pricing without a verification date.  
* Claim full testing when testing was partial.  
* Submit a brief without evidence mapping.  
* Give the writer vague instructions such as “write helpful content.”  
* Move the task to writing before approval.

---

# **32\. Escalation Rules**

Escalate to the Project Lead when:

* Search intent differs significantly from the approved topic.  
* The primary keyword does not support the planned content format.  
* Research findings contradict the planned recommendation.  
* Competing content is significantly stronger than expected.  
* The article cannot provide a meaningful unique value.  
* The app changed pricing or features during brief creation.  
* Important evidence is missing.  
* Technical claims remain unresolved.  
* The content may create legal or reputational risk.  
* A founder requests control over the outline or verdict.  
* The content overlaps with an existing Blinto page.  
* Two teams disagree about the search intent or content angle.  
* The planned CTA conflicts with editorial independence.

The Project Lead may approve a new angle, return the task to research, change the format, combine topics, delay publication, or cancel the content.
