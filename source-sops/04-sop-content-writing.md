# **1\. Purpose**

The purpose of this SOP is to define how Blinto writers turn an approved Shopify app content brief into a complete, accurate, practical, and evidence-based first draft.

**The writing process must ensure that each article:**

* Satisfies the approved search intent.  
* Follows the approved content brief.  
* Uses hands-on testing and research accurately.  
* Helps Shopify merchants make a clear decision.  
* Explains both benefits and limitations.  
* Separates verified findings from app marketing claims.  
* Maintains Blinto’s editorial independence.  
* Supports Blinto’s Shopify topical authority.  
* Includes appropriate internal links and calls to action.  
* Is ready for editorial, SEO, and technical review.

The writer must not begin drafting until SOP 3 has been completed and the content brief has been approved.

---

# **2\. SOP Objective**

**This SOP helps Blinto:**

* Maintain consistent quality across all Shopify app review content.  
* Turn technical app research into useful merchant-focused writing.  
* Prevent unsupported or exaggerated claims.  
* Reduce revision cycles.  
* Present honest strengths, limitations, and recommendations.  
* Create content that is useful beyond a basic feature summary.  
* Demonstrate real Shopify expertise.  
* Protect Blinto’s credibility with merchants and app founders.  
* Create drafts that are structurally and technically ready for review.  
* Build a repeatable content-production process.

---

# **3\. Scope**

**This SOP covers:**

* Reviewing the approved content brief  
* Preparing the draft workspace  
* Following the approved outline  
* Writing the introduction and quick verdict  
* Explaining features and merchant value  
* Presenting hands-on testing evidence  
* Writing pricing, usability, integration, and support sections

* Content-brief creation  
* Final SEO editing  
* Technical fact-checking  
* Graphic production  
* Website publishing  
* Founder outreach

These activities are handled in separate SOPs.

# **8\. Writing Principles**

Every draft must follow these principles.

## **Merchant First**

The article should help the merchant make a decision.

Do not write only for search engines, app founders, or affiliate clicks.

## **Evidence Before Opinion**

Recommendations must be based on:

* Hands-on testing  
* Official documentation  
* Verified pricing  
* Merchant-feedback patterns  
* Technical validation  
* Consistent comparison criteria

## **Balanced Evaluation**

The content should explain:

* What the app does well  
* Where it may fall short  
* Who should use it  
* Who may need another option

## **Clear Decision Support**

The article should help the reader understand:

* Whether the app fits their needs  
* Which plan may be appropriate  
* What setup may involve  
* What limitations to expect  
* Which alternative may be better

## **Editorial Independence**

Blinto’s verdict must not be influenced by:

* Sponsorship  
* Affiliate relationships  
* Founder pressure  
* Partnership opportunities  
* Free access  
* Discounts  
* Gifts  
* Future business discussions

Any relevant commercial relationship must be disclosed.

# **9\. Complete Content Writing Workflow**

## **Step 1: Review the Approved Brief**

**Before writing, the writer must review:**

* Final working title  
* Content type  
* Primary keyword  
* Secondary keywords  
* Target audience  
* Merchant problem  
* Search intent  
* Content angle  
* Unique value proposition  
* Testing level  
* Best-fit merchant  
* Poor-fit merchant  
* Main strengths  
* Important limitations  
* App score  
* Required headings  
* Evidence mapping  
* Internal links  
* CTA direction  
* Visual requirements  
* Claims to avoid  
* Required disclosures  
* Word-count guidance

### **Writer Confirmation**

**Before drafting, the writer should be able to answer:**

* Who is this article for?  
* What decision does the reader need to make?  
* What is the main recommendation?  
* What evidence supports the recommendation?  
* What limitations must be disclosed?  
* What should the reader do after reading?

If any of these are unclear, the writer should request clarification before completing the affected section.

---

## **Step 2: Prepare the Draft Document**

**The draft document should include:**

* Final working title  
* Content status  
* Writer name  
* Draft date  
* Content type  
* Primary keyword  
* Testing level  
* Research links  
* Screenshot links  
* Notes for reviewers

### **Recommended Draft Status**

**First Draft – Not Yet Fact-Checked**

This makes it clear that the article has not yet passed final review.

---

## **Step 3: Follow the Approved Structure**

The writer must follow the approved H1, H2, and H3 outline.

**The writer may make small changes for clarity when:**

* Two headings clearly repeat the same point.  
* A subheading is unnecessary.  
* Research supports a more logical order.  
* A section needs a clearer title.

Major structural changes require approval.

**Do not:**

* Add unrelated sections.  
* Remove required decision-making sections.  
* Copy competitor structures.  
* Add headings only to include keywords.  
* Expand the article with filler.

---

## **Step 4: Write the Introduction**

**The introduction should quickly establish:**

1. The merchant problem  
2. What the app or category does  
3. What Blinto evaluated  
4. What decision the article will help the reader make

### **Introduction Requirements**

**The introduction should:**

* Address the reader’s problem immediately.  
* Mention the app or category clearly.  
* Explain whether Blinto tested or researched the app.  
* Preview the main decision criteria.  
* Avoid long background explanations.  
* Avoid unsupported statistics.  
* Avoid generic ecommerce statements.  
* Avoid repeating the full quick verdict.

### **Example Structure**

**Paragraph 1:**  
Explain the merchant problem.

**Paragraph 2:**  
Introduce the app, apps, or category.

**Paragraph 3:**  
Explain what Blinto tested, compared, or verified.

**Paragraph 4:**  
Tell the reader what decision the article will help them make.

# **10\. Write the Quick Verdict**

Single app reviews and comparison articles must include a concise verdict near the beginning.

Category articles should include a quick recommendation summary.

## **Quick Verdict Requirements**

**The verdict should state:**

* Overall recommendation  
* Best-fit merchant  
* Main strength  
* Main limitation  
* Testing level where relevant  
* Alternative direction where necessary

### **Example Formula**

**\[App name\] is a strong option for \[merchant type\] that needs \[main use case\]. Its main advantage is \[strength\], but merchants that require \[missing capability\] may prefer \[alternative or different solution\].**

### **Quick Verdict Rules**

* Keep it concise.  
* Answer the main question directly.  
* Do not hide the main limitation.  
* Do not use exaggerated words.  
* Do not call an app the “best” without clear criteria.  
* Ensure the verdict matches the final conclusion.

---

# **11\. Explain the Review Methodology**

Each article should clearly explain how Blinto evaluated the app or apps.

## **Include Where Relevant**

* Testing date  
* Development-store use  
* Testing level  
* Features tested  
* Features not tested  
* Official sources reviewed  
* Merchant-feedback analysis  
* Technical review involvement  
* Pricing verification date

### **Example**

**Blinto installed the app on a Shopify development store and tested its onboarding, core setup, storefront output, and main merchant workflow. Advanced paid features were evaluated through official documentation and public product information. Pricing was verified on \[date\].**

### **Methodology Rules**

* Do not claim full testing when testing was partial.  
* Do not describe documentation research as hands-on testing.  
* Do not hide paid-feature limitations.  
* Keep the explanation understandable.  
* Add detailed methodology only where it improves trust.

---

# **12\. Explain What the App Does**

The “What Is” section should explain:

* What the app does  
* Which problem it solves  
* Who it is designed for  
* How it fits into a Shopify store  
* Its main use case

### **Writing Rule**

Do not copy the app’s marketing description.

Translate product language into practical merchant value.

### **Weak Version**

**The app provides powerful tools and seamless functionality.**

### **Better Version**

**The app allows Shopify merchants to collect and display product reviews, including photo and video submissions, from a single dashboard.**

---

# **13\. Write Feature Sections**

Feature sections should explain both function and merchant value.

## **Feature Writing Formula**

For each important feature, explain:

1. What the feature does  
2. How it works  
3. What Blinto observed  
4. Why it matters to the merchant  
5. Any limitation or plan restriction

### **Example**

**Automated review requests**

The app can send review-request emails after an order is fulfilled. During testing, the setup process allowed the timing and message template to be adjusted from the dashboard. This can reduce manual follow-up, but advanced customization may require a paid plan.

### **Feature Rules**

* Group related features.  
* Prioritize important decision factors.  
* Do not list every minor feature.  
* Avoid repeating the App Store description.  
* Explain practical use cases.  
* Mention relevant limitations.  
* Clarify paid-plan restrictions.  
* Distinguish tested and untested features.

---

# **14\. Connect Features to Merchant Outcomes**

The writer should explain why features matter.

Use this relationship:

**Feature → Activity → Benefit → Merchant Outcome**

### **Example**

**Automated review requests → Sends follow-up messages after orders → Reduces manual work → Helps the store collect more customer feedback consistently**

### **Important Rule**

Do not guarantee business outcomes.

Use careful language such as:

* Can help  
* May support  
* Is designed to  
* Makes it easier to  
* Reduces the need for  
* Gives merchants the ability to

Avoid:

* Will increase sales  
* Guarantees conversions  
* Automatically grows revenue  
* Eliminates all manual work

---

# **15\. Present Hands-On Testing Evidence**

Hands-on findings should be presented clearly.

## **Use Evidence for:**

* Installation  
* Onboarding  
* Dashboard usability  
* Core task completion  
* Storefront display  
* Mobile experience  
* Integration setup  
* Performance observations  
* Uninstall behavior  
* Error handling

### **Evidence Writing Rules**

Use language such as:

* During Blinto’s test  
* In the development store  
* We were able to  
* The setup required  
* The dashboard displayed  
* The app added  
* The test showed

Avoid making one test sound universal.

### **Example**

**In Blinto’s development-store test, the app’s onboarding checklist made the initial widget setup straightforward. However, the branding controls available on the free plan were limited.**

---

# **16\. Write Installation and Onboarding Sections**

Explain:

* Installation steps  
* Permission requirements  
* External account requirements  
* Billing requirements  
* Time to complete setup  
* Onboarding guidance  
* Technical difficulty  
* Time to first useful result  
* Important setup limitations

### **Merchant-Focused Questions**

* Can a beginner install it?  
* Does it require coding?  
* Is a developer likely to be needed?  
* Are the required permissions clear?  
* Does setup require an external account?  
* Can the merchant see results quickly?

Avoid judging ease of use only by the number of clicks.

---

# **17\. Write Ease-of-Use Sections**

Evaluate:

* Navigation  
* Dashboard clarity  
* Terminology  
* Settings organization  
* Learning curve  
* Help availability  
* Error handling  
* Mobile usability where relevant

### **Strong Usability Writing**

Instead of:

**The app is easy to use.**

Use:

**The main settings are grouped into a clear left-side menu, and the onboarding checklist guides users through the first setup. Advanced automation settings may still require additional learning.**

Ease of use should be explained, not merely scored.

---

# **18\. Write Pricing and Value Sections**

Pricing information must come from verified sources.

## **Include:**

* Free plan  
* Free trial  
* Entry-level plan  
* Higher-tier plans  
* Usage-based fees  
* Order-based fees  
* Revenue-based charges  
* Important feature restrictions  
* Additional integration costs  
* Enterprise pricing where relevant  
* Pricing verification date

### **Pricing Writing Rules**

* Explain value in context.  
* Identify which merchant type fits each plan.  
* Mention growth-related cost increases.  
* Avoid calling pricing cheap or expensive without context.  
* Do not recommend a plan without explaining why.  
* Clearly separate current pricing from possible future changes.

### **Example**

**The free plan covers the core review-collection features and may be enough for smaller stores. Merchants that need advanced customization, integrations, or higher automation limits may need the paid plan. Pricing was verified on \[date\].**

---

# **19\. Write Integration Sections**

For relevant integrations, explain:

* What the integration connects  
* Why the merchant may need it  
* Whether it is native  
* Whether Blinto tested it  
* Which plan is required  
* Any important setup limitations

### **Integration Rules**

Do not say an integration works well unless it was tested or strongly supported by evidence.

Use:

* The app lists an integration with  
* Official documentation confirms  
* Blinto tested the integration with  
* The integration was not tested

Avoid creating a long list of integration names without explaining relevance.

---

# **20\. Write Performance and Technical Sections**

Technical sections require careful wording.

## **Possible Topics**

* Theme compatibility  
* Script loading  
* App embeds  
* Mobile behavior  
* Storefront speed  
* Theme code  
* Shopify Plus requirements  
* Checkout limitations  
* API access  
* Uninstall behavior  
* App conflicts

### **Technical Writing Rules**

* State the test environment.  
* Explain the testing method.  
* Avoid universal conclusions.  
* Mention limitations.  
* Use approved technical findings only.  
* Flag unresolved claims for technical review.

### **Example**

**In the development-store test, the app added a storefront widget through a theme app extension. No major display issue appeared on the test theme, but merchants using heavily customized themes may require additional configuration.**

---

# **21\. Write Merchant-Feedback Sections**

Merchant feedback should be summarized as patterns.

## **Include:**

* Common positive patterns  
* Common complaints  
* Recent review trends  
* Setup experiences  
* Support experiences  
* Pricing concerns  
* Reliability concerns  
* Feature requests

### **Merchant-Feedback Rules**

* Do not present merchant opinions as verified facts.  
* Do not copy long reviews.  
* Do not focus only on negative feedback.  
* Mention when feedback may refer to older versions.  
* Separate Blinto’s findings from merchant reports.

### **Example**

**Recent Shopify App Store reviews commonly praise the support team’s responsiveness. Critical reviews more often mention pricing changes and difficulties with specific integrations. These reports may not reflect every merchant’s experience.**

---

# **22\. Write Best-Fit and Poor-Fit Guidance**

Every article should clearly explain who the app suits.

## **Best-Fit Guidance**

**Consider:**

* Store size  
* Business model  
* Technical skill  
* Budget  
* Shopify plan  
* Required integrations  
* Growth stage  
* Operational needs

## **Poor-Fit Guidance**

Explain when the app may not be appropriate.

### **Example**

**Best for:**  
Growing Shopify stores that need advanced review collection and visual UGC tools.

**May not be ideal for:**  
Very small stores that only need a basic review widget and want to avoid additional monthly costs.

This guidance must reflect the research.

---

# **23\. Write Pros and Cons**

Pros and cons must be:

* Specific  
* Balanced  
* Evidence-based  
* Merchant-focused  
* Relevant to the decision

## **Strong Pros**

* Guided onboarding reduces the technical effort required for initial setup.  
* The free plan includes the core review-collection workflow.  
* Native integration with \[platform\] supports a connected marketing workflow.

## **Strong Cons**

* Advanced branding controls require a paid plan.  
* Some integrations are limited to higher pricing tiers.  
* The dashboard may feel complex for merchants needing only basic functionality.

## **Avoid**

* Great features  
* Easy to use  
* Good app  
* Expensive  
* Limited functionality

Each point should explain what makes it a strength or limitation.

---

# **24\. Write Alternatives Sections**

Alternatives should be directly relevant.

## **For Each Alternative, Explain:**

* Main difference  
* Best-fit merchant  
* Key strength  
* Key limitation  
* Why someone may choose it instead

### **Alternative Writing Rule**

Do not include alternatives only to add keywords.

### **Example**

**Loox may be a better alternative for merchants prioritizing visual photo reviews and referral features, while Judge.me may offer stronger value for stores that need a more affordable review-collection system.**

---

# **25\. Write Category Review Content**

Category review articles must help merchants compare multiple apps.

## **Category Article Requirements**

**The article should include:**

* Selection methodology  
* Quick comparison table  
* Clear recommendations by use case  
* Consistent app evaluation  
* Pricing summary  
* Main strength  
* Important limitation  
* Testing level  
* Best-fit merchant  
* Clear final shortlist

## **Individual App Summary Format**

**Each app section should include:**

1. What it does  
2. Best for  
3. Key features  
4. Pricing  
5. Main strength  
6. Important limitation  
7. Testing level  
8. Short verdict

### **Category Review Rules**

* Do not describe every app as the best.  
* Do not give identical recommendations.  
* Do not rank apps without criteria.  
* Do not include weak apps only to increase list size.  
* Explain why each app was selected.  
* Keep evaluation criteria consistent.

---

# **26\. Write Comparison Articles**

Comparison articles must evaluate both apps fairly.

## **Comparison Requirements**

**Use the same criteria for both apps:**

* Main use case  
* Features  
* Installation  
* Onboarding  
* Ease of use  
* Pricing  
* Customization  
* Integrations  
* Support  
* Scalability  
* Merchant fit

## **Winner-by-Use-Case Format**

**Identify:**

* Best for beginners  
* Best for budget-conscious merchants  
* Best for advanced features  
* Best for customization  
* Best for growing stores  
* Best for specific integrations

### **Comparison Rules**

* Compare similar pricing levels.  
* Do not give one app more detailed coverage.  
* Do not force a universal winner.  
* Explain when each app is better.  
* Ensure the quick verdict matches the detailed comparison.  
* Use identical scoring criteria.

---

Tables should simplify decisions.

## **Possible Tables**

* Quick verdict  
* Feature comparison  
* Pricing comparison  
* Best app by use case  
* Pros and cons  
* Score summary  
* App alternatives

### **Table Rules**

* Keep entries concise.  
* Verify all facts.  
* Use consistent terms.  
* Do not overcrowd the table.  
* Explain important details in the body.  
* Include pricing dates where relevant.  
* Do not use unexplained scores.

---

# **28\. Use Keywords Naturally**

The primary keyword should appear naturally in:

* H1  
* Introduction  
* One relevant heading  
* Body content  
* Final verdict  
* Metadata where directed

Secondary keywords should appear only where relevant.

### **Keyword Rules**

**Do not:**

* Repeat the same keyword excessively.  
* Force exact-match keywords into awkward sentences.  
* Create duplicate headings.  
* Add unrelated questions.  
* Replace natural language with search-engine language.

The article should satisfy the topic, not count keyword mentions.

---

The writer should include relevant:

* App names  
* Developer names  
* Shopify features  
* Integrations  
* App categories  
* Technical terms  
* Merchant use cases  
* Competitors

### **Entity Writing Rules**

* Use correct product names.  
* Explain relationships.  
* Avoid unnecessary brand lists.  
* Do not include entities only for SEO.  
* Verify technical terms.  
* Use abbreviations only after defining them where needed.

## **Link Types**

* Category page  
* Single app review  
* Comparison article  
* Educational guide  
* Shopify service page  
* Relevant case study

### **Internal-Link Rules**

* Link where the topic is contextually relevant.

# **31\. Write the CTA**

The CTA should connect naturally to the article.

## **CTA Requirements**

* Relevant to the reader’s problem  
* Connected to an appropriate Blinto service  
* Helpful rather than aggressive  
* Placed after the final verdict  
* Supported by short explanatory copy

### **Example**

**Need help selecting, integrating, or improving your Shopify app stack? Blinto can evaluate your store requirements, configure the right tools, and support custom Shopify integrations.**

### **CTA Rules**

Do not:

* Interrupt the article repeatedly.  
* Use unrelated sales offers.  
* Claim that Blinto can guarantee app performance.  
* Weaken the article’s independence.

# **36\. Claims and Wording Restrictions**

The writer must not use absolute claims without strong evidence.

## **Avoid Claims Such As:**

* This app will increase sales.  
* This app is the best Shopify app.  
* This app never slows down stores.  
* This app is completely secure.  
* This app works with every Shopify theme.  
* This app has perfect support.  
* This app is suitable for every merchant.  
* This app guarantees better conversions.

## **Safer Alternatives**

**Use:**

* Can support  
* May help  
* Is designed to  
* In Blinto’s test  
* Based on available documentation  
* Merchant reviews commonly report  
* May be suitable for  
* Appears to work best for  
* Results will depend on implementation

---

# **37\. Originality Requirements**

The draft must be original.

**Do not:**

* Copy app descriptions.  
* Copy competitor headings.  
* Copy merchant reviews.  
* Rewrite another article sentence by sentence.  
* Use AI-generated claims without verification.  
* Add fabricated experience.  
* Create fake quotes.  
* Present public information as Blinto’s testing.

The writer should use the approved research to create an original explanation and evaluation.

---

# **38\. Word-Count Guidance**

Word count should support the topic without filler.

| Content Type | Suggested Range |
| ----- | ----- |
| Single App Review | 1,800–3,000 words |
| Comparison Article | 2,000–3,500 words |
| Category Review | 2,500–4,500 words |

These are guidelines.

A shorter article is acceptable when it fully satisfies the intent. A longer article should not repeat information.

# **39\. Writer Self-Review**

Before submission, the writer must complete a self-review.

## **Structure Check**

* The H1 matches the approved brief.  
* All required sections are included.  
* Heading order is logical.  
* No unnecessary sections were added.  
* No required decision section is missing.  
* The introduction and conclusion are not repetitive.

## **Search Intent Check**

* The main question is answered early.  
* The article helps the reader make a decision.  
* The primary keyword fits naturally.  
* Secondary keywords are used only where relevant.  
* The content does not drift into unrelated topics.

## **Evidence Check**

* Testing claims match the documented testing level.  
* Official claims are identified correctly.  
* Pricing is verified and dated.  
* Merchant feedback is presented as reported experience.  
* Technical claims are marked for review.  
* No unsupported outcome claims are included.

## **Merchant Value Check**

* Features are connected to practical value.  
* Best-fit merchants are identified.  
* Poor-fit merchants are identified.  
* Important limitations are visible.  
* Alternatives are relevant.  
* The final verdict is clear.

## **Writing Quality Check**

* Paragraphs are concise.  
* Sentences are easy to understand.  
* Repetition has been removed.  
* Marketing language has been reduced.  
* Technical terms are explained.  
* Tables are clear.  
* Pros and cons are specific.

## **Conversion Check**

* Internal links are included or marked.  
* CTA is relevant.  
* CTA does not weaken editorial trust.  
* Visual placeholders are complete.  
* Required disclosures are included.

# **43\. Common Mistakes to Avoid**

**Do not:**

* Start writing before the brief is approved.  
* Ignore the testing level.  
* Copy app marketing language.  
* Copy competitor content.  
* Turn the article into a feature list.  
* Use unsupported outcome claims.  
* Hide app limitations.  
* Write generic pros and cons.  
* Call an app the best without clear criteria.  
* Force a universal winner in comparison content.  
* Use outdated pricing.  
* Present merchant feedback as fact.  
* Add irrelevant keywords.  
* Repeat the same recommendation in several sections.  
* Use aggressive sales CTAs.  
* Forget testing disclosures.  
* Forget pricing verification dates.  
* Leave technical claims unmarked.  
* Submit without self-review.  
* Move the task to review with missing sections.

# **44\. Escalation Rules**

Escalate to the Content Lead, SEO Lead, or Project Lead when:

* The approved verdict conflicts with the evidence.  
* The writer finds major factual gaps.  
* The app changes during writing.  
* Pricing changes significantly.  
* The approved keyword no longer matches the content.  
* The article overlaps with an existing Blinto page.  
* A technical claim cannot be explained safely.  
* The founder requests changes to the verdict.  
* The article may create legal or reputational risk.  
* The research is too weak to support a recommendation.  
* The writer believes the content format should change.  
* The required word count creates unnecessary repetition.  
* The CTA conflicts with editorial independence.

The Project Lead may approve additional research, brief revision, content restructuring, app replacement, delay, or cancellation.
