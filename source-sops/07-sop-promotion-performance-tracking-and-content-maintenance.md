# **1. Purpose**

The purpose of this SOP is to define how Blinto promotes published Shopify app review content, tracks organic and business performance, builds relationships with app founders, maintains content accuracy, updates outdated information, and decides whether the project should be scaled, improved, narrowed, or paused.

Publishing an article is not the end of the process. Each Shopify app review must continue to be managed as a long-term business and SEO asset.

This SOP ensures that Blinto:

- Gives each published review an initial promotion plan.

- Notifies relevant app founders without compromising editorial independence.

- Tracks organic visibility, engagement, leads, and partnership opportunities.

- Compares the performance of different content formats.

- Monitors pricing, features, ratings, screenshots, and app availability.

- Updates content before it becomes misleading or outdated.

- Identifies content decay and ranking opportunities.

- Connects performance with Blinto’s business objectives.

- Learns from each published article.

- Makes evidence-based decisions about scaling the project.

# **2. SOP Objective**

This SOP helps Blinto:

- Generate more visibility from each published article.

- Build relationships with Shopify app founders and companies.

- Create backlink, mention, collaboration, and partnership opportunities.

- Measure whether the project generates meaningful organic traffic.

- Track qualified leads and business opportunities.

- Understand which app categories perform best.

- Compare category reviews, single-app reviews, and comparison articles.

- Identify weak content early.

- Keep reviews accurate and trustworthy.

- Build a repeatable content-refresh process.

- Improve internal knowledge of the Shopify ecosystem.

- Decide how the 100-content target should be executed.

# **10. Initial Promotion**

- Content team

- Shopify developers

- Sales team

- Business development

- Account management

### **Internal Distribution Purpose**

- Build internal Shopify knowledge.

- Allow the team to use the article during sales conversations.

- Identify technical or factual issues.

- Create internal-linking opportunities.

- Generate service and partnership ideas.

- Help team members understand the reviewed app category.

### **Internal Distribution Message Should Include**

- Article title

- Live URL

- Main audience

- Main verdict

- Relevant Blinto service

- Possible sales or partnership use

- Request to report factual or technical issues

## **Step 3: Add Internal Links From Existing Content**

After publication, identify existing pages that should link to the new article.

### **Possible Source Pages**

- Shopify app category pages

- Other app reviews

- Comparison articles

- Shopify guides

- Service pages

- Relevant case studies

- Existing Shopify blog content

### **Internal-Linking Rules**

- Use contextually relevant anchor text.

- Do not force links from unrelated pages.

- Prioritize pages already receiving traffic.

- Add links in sections where readers need the information.

- Record each added link.

- Recheck links after URL changes.

### **Minimum Requirement**

The article should receive at least one relevant internal link shortly after publication whenever suitable source content exists.

The article should not remain orphaned.

## **Step 4: Share Through Approved Social Channels**

Create platform-appropriate promotional content.

### **Possible Channels**

- LinkedIn

- Facebook

- X

- Shopify-related communities where permitted

- Blinto team member profiles

- Relevant professional groups

- Internal Slack or company channels

### **Social Promotion Should Focus On**

- Merchant problem

- Main comparison or finding

- Best-fit use case

- Important limitation

- Practical takeaway

- Link to the full review

### **Social Promotion Rules**

- Do not present the review as sponsored unless it is.

- Do not tag founders aggressively.

- Do not claim that the app is universally best.

- Do not hide limitations.

- Do not post the same message repeatedly.

- Follow community rules.

- Avoid spammy distribution.

## **Step 5: Include Relevant Content in the Newsletter**

Where appropriate, include the article in:

- Blinto’s newsletter

- Shopify-focused email updates

- Curated Shopify app resources

- Monthly content roundup

- Internal knowledge email

### **Newsletter Positioning**

The article should be positioned as:

- A merchant decision guide

- An evidence-based review

- A practical comparison

- A category recommendation

- A new Shopify ecosystem resource

Newsletter promotion should prioritize useful insights over direct sales language.

# **11. Founder and App-Company Outreach**

## **Step 6: Determine Founder-Outreach Eligibility**

Founder outreach is optional and should only be used when it creates a legitimate relationship opportunity.

### **Eligible Situations**

- A detailed single-app review has been published.

- The app is included prominently in a category review.

- The app is compared fairly with a direct competitor.

- Blinto has completed meaningful hands-on research.

- The article contains useful feedback.

- The app founder or team is relevant to Blinto’s business goals.

- There is a potential collaboration, backlink, interview, or partnership opportunity.

### **Do Not Conduct Outreach When**

- The article contains unresolved concerns.

- The app appears inactive or untrustworthy.

- Serious security or reputation concerns exist.

- The article was based on insufficient evidence.

- Outreach may create legal or editorial risk.

- The only purpose is to request a backlink.

## **Step 7: Research the Correct Contact**

Possible contacts include:

- Founder

- Co-founder

- Marketing lead

- Partnerships lead

- Content lead

- Product lead

- Support contact

- Official company email

Use professional and publicly available business contact information.

Do not collect or use private personal contact data inappropriately.

## **Step 8: Send the Initial Outreach**

The outreach should:

- Introduce Blinto briefly.

- Mention the published review.

- Share the live link.

- Explain the evaluation approach.

- Invite factual corrections.

- Make it clear that editorial decisions remain with Blinto.

- Avoid demanding a backlink.

- Avoid requesting a positive response.

- Avoid implying endorsement.

### **Suggested Outreach Structure**

1.  Brief introduction

2.  Reason for contacting

3.  Article link

4.  Short explanation of testing or research

5.  Invitation to report factual inaccuracies

6.  Optional collaboration or discussion invitation

7.  Clear editorial independence statement where needed

### **Important Rule**

Founders may suggest factual corrections, but they should not control:

- Scores

- Pros and cons

- Rankings

- Comparison winners

- Final verdict

- Publication status

## **Step 9: Handle Founder Responses**

Founder responses should be classified.

| **Response Type**         | **Required Action**                        |
|---------------------------|--------------------------------------------|
| Positive acknowledgment   | Record the response                        |
| Factual correction        | Verify before updating                     |
| Product update            | Confirm through official evidence          |
| Backlink or social share  | Track as earned exposure                   |
| Partnership interest      | Send to Business Development               |
| Interview opportunity     | Evaluate strategically                     |
| Sponsorship request       | Follow disclosure and independence rules   |
| Disagreement with opinion | Review, but do not change without evidence |
| Request for removal       | Escalate to Project Lead                   |
| Legal complaint           | Escalate immediately                       |

### **Factual Correction Rule**

Do not update the article only because the founder requests it.

Verify the correction through:

- Official documentation

- Current product access

- Updated pricing page

- Changelog

- Technical retesting

- Reliable supporting evidence

# **12. Search Performance Tracking**

## **Step 11: Establish the Search Baseline**

Record the article’s starting performance.

### **Baseline Fields**

- Publication date

- Indexed date

- Primary keyword ranking

- Secondary keyword rankings

- Existing impressions

- Existing clicks

- Existing CTR

- Average position

- Referring internal links

- External backlinks

- Search-result features

- Competing pages

For new content, some baseline values may be zero.

## **Step 12: Monitor Indexing**

Check whether:

- The page has been discovered.

- The page is indexed.

- Google selected the intended canonical.

- The page is not blocked.

- The page appears in the sitemap.

- The page is linked internally.

- No duplicate URL competes with it.

### **Indexing Review Schedule**

Recommended checks:

- Within the first week

- At approximately 14 days

- At approximately 30 days

- When an indexing issue appears

Do not repeatedly request indexing without investigating the underlying issue.

## **Step 13: Monitor Search Queries**

Review the queries that generate impressions and clicks.

### **Evaluate**

- Primary keyword

- App name plus review

- App name plus pricing

- App name plus alternatives

- App A versus App B

- Best app category queries

- Long-tail merchant questions

- Unexpected queries

- Irrelevant queries

- Queries ranking on page two or three

### **Search-Query Opportunities**

A query may indicate that the article needs:

- A clearer answer

- A stronger heading

- A pricing update

- A comparison section

- An FAQ answer

- A new supporting article

- A dedicated comparison article

- Better internal links

## **Step 14: Monitor Impressions, Clicks, and CTR**

Track:

- Total impressions

- Total clicks

- CTR

- Average position

- Branded versus non-branded queries

- Device performance

- Country performance where relevant

- Page-level growth

### **Interpretation Examples**

#### **Impressions Increasing, Clicks Low**

Possible actions:

- Improve SEO title.

- Improve meta description.

- Reassess search intent.

- Strengthen the direct answer.

- Review competing search-result titles.

#### **Good Position, Low CTR**

Possible actions:

- Improve title clarity.

- Add the relevant decision or use case.

- Remove generic wording.

- Align the title with current search intent.

#### **Low Impressions**

Possible actions:

- Improve internal linking.

- Check indexation.

- Reassess keyword opportunity.

- Expand semantic relevance.

- Build supporting content.

- Review whether the topic has enough demand.

## **Step 15: Monitor Keyword Rankings**

Track:

- Primary keyword

- Important secondary keywords

- Comparison terms

- Pricing terms

- Alternatives terms

- App-category queries

- Relevant question keywords

### **Ranking Segments**

| **Position** | **General Interpretation**                      |
|--------------|-------------------------------------------------|
| 1–3          | Strong visibility                               |
| 4–10         | First-page opportunity                          |
| 11–20        | High-priority optimization opportunity          |
| 21–50        | Needs stronger relevance or authority           |
| 51+          | Early or weak visibility                        |
| Not ranking  | Reassess indexing, intent, relevance, or demand |

Rankings should be interpreted with traffic, intent, and business value.

# **17. Content Maintenance**

## **Step 26: Monitor Time-Sensitive Information**

Shopify app review content may become outdated because of changes to:

- Pricing

- Free plans

- Free trials

- Features

- Integrations

- Ratings

- Review counts

- App name

- Developer name

- Ownership

- Dashboard design

- Shopify compatibility

- Shopify plan requirements

- Support options

- Privacy terms

- App Store availability

The app-research owner should monitor critical information.

## **Step 27: Set Content Refresh Frequency**

Recommended review frequency:

| **Content Type**           | **Standard Review Frequency** |
|----------------------------|-------------------------------|
| Single App Review          | Every 3–6 months              |
| Comparison Article         | Every 3–6 months              |
| Category Review            | Every 2–4 months              |
| High-Traffic Article       | More frequently when needed   |
| Fast-Changing App          | Event-based or more frequent  |
| Low-Traffic Stable Article | Every 6–12 months             |

This schedule may change based on:

- App update frequency

- Traffic level

- Lead value

- Ranking position

- Founder notification

- Pricing changes

- Shopify platform changes

## **Step 28: Create Refresh Triggers**

A content-refresh task should be created when:

- Pricing changes.

- A free plan is added or removed.

- Important features are added or removed.

- The app is rebranded.

- The developer changes.

- The app is acquired.

- The app is removed from the Shopify App Store.

- Screenshots become outdated.

- Ratings change significantly.

- Integrations change.

- Shopify plan requirements change.

- The article loses rankings.

- Search impressions decline.

- CTR declines.

- Competitors publish stronger information.

- Merchant questions change.

- The article receives factual feedback.

- The CTA or internal links become outdated.

- A new direct competitor becomes important.

## **Step 29: Complete the Refresh Scorecard**

Score each article from 1 to 5.

| **Factor**                 | **Description**                            |
|----------------------------|--------------------------------------------|
| Traffic Value              | Current or potential organic traffic       |
| Business Value             | Lead, partnership, or service potential    |
| Ranking Opportunity        | Ability to improve search visibility       |
| Information Change         | Amount of outdated information             |
| Content Decay              | Decline in impressions, clicks, or ranking |
| App Importance             | Importance of the app or category          |
| Update Effort              | Resources required                         |
| Content Cluster Value      | Importance to connected pages              |
| Founder Relationship Value | Partnership or relationship importance     |
| Risk of Inaccuracy         | Potential harm from outdated claims        |

### **Priority Levels**

| **Score**   | **Priority**      |
|-------------|-------------------|
| 41–50       | Immediate refresh |
| 31–40       | High priority     |
| 21–30       | Medium priority   |
| 11–20       | Low priority      |
| 10 or below | Monitor only      |

The team may prioritize a lower-scoring page when a factual or reputational issue exists.

# **19. Content Decay Monitoring**

## **Step 35: Identify Content Decay**

Possible decay signals include:

- Declining impressions

- Declining clicks

- Ranking loss

- Lower CTR

- Reduced engagement

- Fewer CTA clicks

- Competitors overtaking the page

- New search intent

- Outdated screenshots

- Broken links

- Missing app options

- Weak topical coverage

### **Comparison Periods**

Review:

- Month over month

- Quarter over quarter

- Same period where seasonal behavior exists

- Before and after major updates

Do not treat short-term fluctuation as confirmed decay without context.

## **Step 36: Diagnose the Cause**

Possible causes include:

- Outdated pricing

- New competitors

- Search-intent changes

- Weak title or description

- Reduced demand

- App popularity decline

- Content quality issues

- Thin comparison coverage

- Weak internal links

- Lost backlinks

- Technical indexing issue

- Cannibalization

- Shopify ecosystem changes

The update plan should address the actual cause.

# **20. App Rebrand, Acquisition, Removal, or Closure**

## **Step 37: Handle App Rebrands**

When an app changes its name:

- Confirm the official new name.

- Check whether the URL changed.

- Update the title and article.

- Add the former name where useful.

- Update screenshots.

- Update internal links.

- Update metadata.

- Update the knowledge base.

- Consider whether a redirect is required.

## **Step 38: Handle App Acquisitions**

When an app is acquired:

- Confirm the acquisition.

- Review product or pricing changes.

- Review support and company information.

- Update ownership details.

- Avoid assuming product quality changed.

- Monitor future changes.

- Update disclosures if a relationship exists.

## **Step 39: Handle App Removal or Closure**

When an app is removed or discontinued:

### **Possible Actions**

- Update the article with a clear notice.

- Recommend alternatives.

- Remove the app from category rankings.

- Update comparison articles.

- Redirect the page to a relevant alternative guide.

- Archive the article.

- Keep the page live as a historical resource when useful.

### **Decision Factors**

- Existing traffic

- Existing backlinks

- User intent

- Availability of alternatives

- Historical value

- Legal or reputational concerns

- Content-cluster impact

Do not leave a discontinued app presented as available.

# **21. Cannibalization and Overlap Review**

## **Step 40: Monitor Content Overlap**

Review whether multiple Blinto pages compete for the same search intent.

Possible examples:

- A category article and a guide target the same keyword.

- Two reviews target the same app-review query.

- A comparison article overlaps with both single app pages.

- Old and new URLs cover the same topic.

### **Possible Actions**

- Differentiate the intent.

- Merge overlapping content.

- Update internal links.

- Change the primary keyword.

- Redirect the weaker page.

- Use canonicalization only when strategically appropriate.

Do not allow several pages to provide nearly identical content.

# **23. Validation Decision Framework**

## **Step 43: Score the Project**

Evaluate the project from 1 to 5 across:

| **Area**              | **Description**                             |
|-----------------------|---------------------------------------------|
| Organic Visibility    | Impressions and ranking growth              |
| Organic Traffic       | Relevant clicks and sessions                |
| Search Intent Fit     | Quality of queries and engagement           |
| Merchant Engagement   | Scroll, internal links, and CTA activity    |
| Lead Generation       | Qualified inquiries                         |
| Founder Engagement    | Responses, shares, and relationships        |
| Partnership Potential | Collaborations and commercial opportunities |
| Backlink Potential    | Earned links and mentions                   |
| Internal Learning     | Shopify ecosystem expertise                 |
| Production Efficiency | Time and resource requirements              |
| Content Maintenance   | Ongoing update burden                       |
| Strategic Fit         | Support for Blinto’s positioning            |

### **Maximum Score**

**60 points**

### **Decision Guide**

| **Score**   | **Decision**                             |
|-------------|------------------------------------------|
| 49–60       | Scale                                    |
| 37–48       | Improve and scale selectively            |
| 25–36       | Narrow to stronger categories or formats |
| 13–24       | Pause and reassess                       |
| 12 or below | Stop or redesign the initiative          |

Scores should support, not replace, leadership judgment.

## **Step 44: Make the Final Project Decision**

### **Scale**

Choose this when:

- Organic visibility is growing.

- Relevant traffic is increasing.

- Strong categories are emerging.

- Founder or merchant engagement exists.

- Production is manageable.

- The project supports Blinto’s positioning.

### **Improve**

Choose this when:

- The idea shows potential.

- Content quality or promotion needs improvement.

- Rankings exist but clicks are limited.

- CTA or conversion paths are weak.

- Production is inefficient.

### **Narrow**

Choose this when:

- Only certain categories perform.

- One content format significantly outperforms others.

- Partnership value is concentrated in a few app types.

- Maintenance requirements are too high across all categories.

### **Pause**

Choose this when:

- Search demand is weaker than expected.

- Content cannot compete effectively.

- Production costs are excessive.

- No meaningful business signals appear.

- The project needs a new strategy.

### **Archive or Stop**

Choose this when:

- The project creates no strategic value.

- Content cannot be maintained accurately.

- Apps or categories have become irrelevant.

- Resources have a substantially better use elsewhere.

# **26. Quality Standards**

- Every article has a performance owner.

- Published content receives appropriate initial promotion.

- Founder feedback does not compromise editorial independence.

- Search performance is tracked consistently.

- Leads and partnerships are attributed where possible.

- Pricing and feature changes are identified.

- Outdated content is refreshed.

- App closures and rebrands are handled correctly.

- Content-decay causes are diagnosed.

- Monthly and quarterly reports produce actions.

- The internal Shopify app knowledge base remains current.

# **27. Common Mistakes to Avoid**

Do not:

- Treat publication as the end of the process.

- Judge SEO performance after only a few days.

- Contact founders only to request backlinks.

- Allow founders to control the verdict.

- Update content without verifying corrections.

- Track traffic without tracking leads or relationships.

- Combine all content formats in one performance category.

- Ignore low-traffic content with high commercial value.

- Ignore high-traffic content that generates no business value.

- Update visible dates without meaningful changes.

- Leave outdated pricing in published articles.

- Leave discontinued apps in current recommendation lists.

- Change URLs without a redirect plan.

- Create multiple pages with the same search intent.

- Refresh content without diagnosing the reason for decline.

- Overpromote articles in communities.

- Use spammy outreach.

- Ignore mobile engagement.

- Track backlinks only by quantity.

- Scale to 100 articles before reviewing validation results.

# **28. Escalation Rules**

Escalate to the Project Lead when:

- A founder disputes a significant factual claim.

- A legal complaint is received.

- An app is removed or discontinued.

- A serious security concern appears.

- The article contains materially outdated information.

- A founder offers payment for favorable changes.

- A partnership may affect editorial independence.

- Rankings decline significantly across a cluster.

- Multiple pages compete for the same intent.

- A high-traffic URL requires redirection.

- The project generates traffic but no relevant business value.

- The project generates leads but content accuracy cannot be maintained.

- Maintenance requirements exceed available capacity.

- Leadership must choose between scaling and narrowing the project.
