# **1\. Purpose**

The purpose of this SOP is to define how Blinto reviews, corrects, validates, and approves Shopify app review content before it moves to design and publishing.

This review process ensures that every article:

* Follows the approved content brief.  
* Satisfies the intended search intent.  
* Provides useful merchant-focused information.  
* Uses Shopify app research correctly.  
* Presents hands-on testing honestly.  
* Contains accurate pricing, features, and technical information.  
* Uses balanced recommendations.  
* Includes appropriate internal links and calls to action.  
* Avoids unsupported, exaggerated, or misleading claims.  
* Meets Blinto’s editorial, SEO, technical, and trust standards.  
* Is ready for design, formatting, and publication.

This SOP must be completed before the content is considered approved.

---

# **2\. SOP Objective**

This SOP helps Blinto:

* Maintain consistent content quality.  
* Protect Blinto’s credibility.  
* Reduce factual and technical errors.  
* Ensure that testing evidence is represented honestly.  
* Improve SEO performance without keyword stuffing.  
* Keep content aligned with search intent.  
* Prevent repetitive, generic, or promotional writing.  
* Improve content usefulness for Shopify merchants.  
* Standardize revisions and approvals.  
* Reduce errors during publishing.  
* Establish clear responsibility for editorial, SEO, and technical validation.

---

# **9\. Complete Review Workflow**

## **Step 1: Conduct the Draft Intake Check**

Before detailed review, the Content Lead or Project Manager confirms that the submission is complete.

### **Check:**

* The full draft is attached.  
* The approved brief is attached.  
* Research and evidence links work.  
* The content type is correct.  
* The writer self-review is complete.  
* Word count is recorded.  
* Testing level is recorded.  
* Pricing verification date is included.  
* Visual placeholders are present.  
* Internal-link placeholders are present.  
* Writer notes are attached.  
* Technical review sections are identified.  
* Required disclosures are included.

### **Intake Decisions**

| Decision | Meaning |
| ----- | ----- |
| Accepted for Review | The draft is complete |
| Returned to Writer | Important parts or attachments are missing |
| Escalated | The draft contains a serious strategic or factual issue |

The draft should not move forward until the intake requirements are complete.

---

## **Step 2: Review Compliance With the Approved Brief**

The editor must compare the draft with the approved content brief.

### **Confirm:**

* The content format matches the brief.  
* The target audience is clear.  
* The merchant problem is addressed.  
* The primary search intent is satisfied.  
* The approved content angle is maintained.  
* Required sections are included.  
* The quick verdict matches the approved direction.  
* Required evidence is used.  
* Best-fit and poor-fit merchants are identified.  
* Required tables are included.  
* Internal links are included or marked.  
* CTA direction is followed.  
* Claims to avoid are not included.  
* Required disclosures are present.  
* Word count is within a reasonable range.

### **Brief Deviation Categories**

| Category | Example |
| ----- | ----- |
| Minor | Heading wording changed without affecting meaning |
| Moderate | Required section is too short or incomplete |
| Major | Verdict, format, or search intent changed |
| Critical | Unsupported claims or fabricated testing were added |

Major or critical deviations require revision before other review stages continue.

---

# **10\. Editorial Review**

## **Step 3: Review Content Structure**

The Content Lead should confirm that the article has a logical structure.

### **Check:**

* The H1 matches the topic.  
* The introduction addresses the merchant problem.  
* The quick verdict appears early.  
* Sections follow the reader’s decision process.  
* Similar topics are grouped.  
* Repetitive sections are merged.  
* Important limitations are easy to find.  
* The conclusion provides a clear decision.  
* The CTA appears after useful information.  
* The article does not contain unnecessary sections.

### **Recommended Decision Flow**

A strong review article should generally help the reader move through:

**Problem → App understanding → Testing evidence → Features → Pricing → Merchant fit → Limitations → Alternatives → Final decision**

The exact order may vary by content format.

---

## **Step 4: Review Clarity and Readability**

The editor should check whether the content is understandable to the intended audience.

### **Review:**

* Sentence length  
* Paragraph length  
* Technical terminology  
* Heading clarity  
* Transitions  
* Explanation depth  
* Table readability  
* Repetition  
* Unnecessary jargon  
* Robotic phrasing  
* Promotional wording

### **Editing Rules**

The editor should:

* Shorten long paragraphs.  
* Simplify complicated sentences.  
* Explain technical terms.  
* Remove filler.  
* Replace vague claims with specific observations.  
* Remove repeated ideas.  
* Strengthen weak transitions.  
* Keep the merchant decision central.

### **Weak Example**

**This powerful application provides a variety of seamless and innovative functionalities designed to transform ecommerce experiences.**

### **Improved Example**

**The app helps Shopify merchants create post-purchase upsell offers and track how those offers perform.**

---

## **Step 5: Review Tone and Editorial Independence**

The article should sound confident, practical, and balanced.

### **Confirm That the Content:**

* Does not sound like app marketing copy.  
* Does not unfairly criticize the app.  
* Does not hide meaningful limitations.  
* Does not use excessive praise.  
* Does not pressure the reader to install the app.  
* Does not prioritize a founder relationship over merchant usefulness.  
* Clearly distinguishes opinion from evidence.  
* Uses fair language when discussing complaints.

### **Prohibited Tone**

Avoid:

* “This revolutionary app is perfect for everyone.”  
* “You must install this app immediately.”  
* “This is easily the best Shopify app available.”  
* “The app is terrible and should be avoided.”

Recommendations should be contextual.

---

## **Step 6: Review the Introduction**

The introduction should:

* Explain the merchant problem.  
* Identify the app or category.  
* Mention the testing or research approach.  
* Explain what decision the article supports.  
* Avoid unnecessary background.  
* Avoid unsupported statistics.  
* Avoid delaying the answer.

The editor should remove introductions that begin with broad statements such as:

* Ecommerce is growing rapidly.  
* Shopify is a popular platform.  
* Choosing apps can be difficult.

The introduction should move directly to the specific merchant decision.

---

## **Step 7: Review the Quick Verdict**

The quick verdict must:

* Answer the main question.  
* Identify the best-fit merchant.  
* Mention the app’s main strength.  
* Mention the main limitation.  
* Match the research.  
* Match the final verdict.  
* Avoid exaggerated wording.

### **Quick Verdict Review Question**

**Could a merchant understand the main recommendation without reading the entire article?**

If not, the verdict should be revised.

---

## **Step 8: Review Feature Explanations**

For each major feature, confirm that the draft explains:

1. What the feature does  
2. How it works  
3. What Blinto observed  
4. Why it matters  
5. Any limitation or plan restriction

The editor should remove feature lists that do not explain merchant value.

### **Weak Feature Writing**

* Automated emails  
* Review widgets  
* Analytics  
* Integrations

### **Better Feature Writing**

**The app can automatically send review-request emails after order fulfillment. This reduces manual follow-up, although advanced template customization is limited to the paid plan.**

---

## **Step 9: Review Merchant Value**

The article should connect features to practical merchant outcomes.

### **Check Whether the Draft Explains:**

* Time savings  
* Setup effort  
* Operational impact  
* Customer experience  
* Marketing workflow  
* Store management  
* Scalability  
* Team requirements  
* Budget impact  
* Integration value

Avoid guaranteed outcome claims.

The content may explain what a feature supports, but it should not promise results without evidence.

---

## **Step 10: Review Pros and Cons**

Pros and cons should be:

* Specific  
* Balanced  
* Supported by research  
* Relevant to merchant decisions  
* Consistent with the article body

### **Remove Generic Statements Such As:**

* Easy to use  
* Good support  
* Great features  
* Expensive  
* Limited options

### **Replace With Specific Statements Such As:**

* The onboarding checklist makes basic widget setup possible without code.  
* Advanced branding controls require the paid plan.  
* Pricing may increase significantly for merchants needing multiple advanced integrations.

---

## **Step 11: Review Best-Fit and Poor-Fit Guidance**

The article must identify:

* Who should consider the app  
* Who may not need it  
* Which merchant stage it fits  
* Which technical skill level it suits  
* Which budget range it fits  
* Which use cases it does not serve well

### **Review Question**

**Does the article help the wrong-fit merchant avoid making a poor decision?**

If not, the guidance should be improved.

---

## **Step 12: Review the Final Verdict**

The final verdict should include:

* Clear recommendation  
* Best-fit merchant  
* Main advantage  
* Main limitation  
* Alternative direction where relevant  
* No new information

The conclusion should not repeat the entire article.

It should help the reader make the final decision.

---

# **11\. SEO Review**

## **Step 13: Validate Search Intent**

The SEO Editor must confirm that the article satisfies the approved search intent.

### **Category Review Intent**

The reader wants to:

* Compare several apps  
* Understand differences  
* Build a shortlist  
* Identify the best option for a use case

### **Single App Review Intent**

The reader wants to:

* Understand the app  
* Evaluate features and pricing  
* Identify limitations  
* Decide whether to install it

### **Comparison Intent**

The reader wants to:

* Compare two apps  
* Understand which is better for a specific use case  
* Make a final decision

### **Search Intent Failure Examples**

* A single app review becomes a general category guide.  
* A category article does not compare apps.  
* A comparison article explains each app separately without a direct comparison.  
* The article gives features but no recommendation.  
* The article targets merchants, but most content focuses on app founders.

If the search intent is not satisfied, structural revision is required.

---

## **Step 14: Review Primary Keyword Alignment**

The SEO Editor should confirm that:

* The primary keyword matches the article.  
* The keyword appears naturally in the H1.  
* The introduction confirms the topic.  
* At least one heading reflects the main topic where natural.  
* The content does not target a different primary query.  
* The conclusion reinforces the decision.  
* Keyword usage is not excessive.

The article should not be judged by keyword density.

---

## **Step 15: Review Secondary Keywords**

Secondary keywords should:

* Match relevant sections.  
* Support the primary topic.  
* Appear naturally.  
* Answer additional merchant questions.  
* Avoid creating duplicate or unnecessary sections.

### **Example**

For a Judge.me review:

* Judge.me pricing  
* Judge.me features  
* Judge.me alternatives  
* Is Judge.me free?  
* Judge.me pros and cons

Each secondary query should be answered in the appropriate section.

---

## **Step 16: Review Semantic Coverage**

The editor should check whether the article covers the concepts needed to answer the topic fully.

Possible semantic areas include:

* App category  
* Core use case  
* Installation  
* Onboarding  
* Features  
* Pricing  
* Integrations  
* Merchant suitability  
* Limitations  
* Support  
* Alternatives  
* Shopify plan requirements  
* Technical considerations  
* Scalability

### **Important Rule**

Semantic coverage does not mean including every related term.

Only include concepts that improve the article’s usefulness.

---

## **Step 17: Review Entity Accuracy**

Confirm:

* App names are correct.  
* Developer names are correct.  
* Integration names are correct.  
* Shopify features are named correctly.  
* Product terminology is consistent.  
* Competitor names are spelled correctly.  
* Technical entities are used accurately.

Avoid adding unrelated app names only for search visibility.

---

## **Step 18: Review Heading Structure**

The heading structure should:

* Contain only one H1.  
* Use H2s for major sections.  
* Use H3s for supporting topics.  
* Follow a logical hierarchy.  
* Avoid duplicate headings.  
* Avoid headings created only for keywords.  
* Make the article easy to scan.

### **Heading Review Example**

Weak:

* Features  
* More Features  
* Other Features  
* Additional Tools

Better:

* Review Collection and Display  
* Customization and Branding  
* Integrations and Automation  
* Analytics and Reporting

---

## **Step 19: Review Direct Answers**

Important reader questions should receive clear answers.

### **Check:**

* The main verdict appears early.  
* Pricing is explained clearly.  
* Free-plan availability is clear.  
* Coding requirements are clear.  
* Best-fit merchant is clear.  
* Comparison winners are clearly identified.  
* FAQ answers are concise.

Avoid hiding answers inside long paragraphs.

---

## **Step 20: Review FAQ Quality**

The FAQ should:

* Address genuine merchant questions.  
* Avoid repeating the main body.  
* Avoid keyword-only questions.  
* Provide direct answers.  
* Match the available evidence.  
* Avoid unsupported promises.

Remove FAQ questions that do not improve the reader’s decision.

---

## **Step 21: Review Metadata Recommendations**

Confirm the draft or brief includes:

* Suggested title tag  
* Suggested meta description  
* Suggested URL slug

### **Title Tag Review**

The title should:

* Match search intent.  
* Include the primary keyword naturally.  
* Clearly describe the article.  
* Avoid clickbait.  
* Avoid unsupported “best” claims.

### **Meta Description Review**

The description should:

* Explain what the reader will learn.  
* Mention the decision or comparison.  
* Be accurate.  
* Avoid exaggerated claims.

### **URL Review**

The slug should be:

* Short  
* Clear  
* Lowercase  
* Consistent with Blinto’s architecture

# **12\. Research and Evidence Validation**

## **Step 22: Verify the Testing Level**

The reviewer must confirm that the article accurately represents the testing level.

| Testing Level | Acceptable Description |
| ----- | ----- |
| Full Hands-On Test | Blinto installed and tested the relevant core features |
| Partial Hands-On Test | Blinto tested available features but not all paid or restricted features |
| Demo and Documentation Review | Blinto reviewed a demo and official documentation |
| Research-Only Evaluation | Blinto used official information and merchant-feedback patterns |
| Insufficient Evidence | Article should not proceed without approval |

### **Critical Rule**

The article must never say:

* “We tested the app”  
* “During our full review”  
* “Our hands-on test showed”

unless the documented research supports those statements.

---

## **Step 23: Verify Evidence Placement**

Check whether claims are supported by the correct evidence.

| Claim Type | Required Evidence |
| ----- | ----- |
| Installation experience | Testing notes and screenshots |
| Pricing | Official pricing source |
| Feature availability | Testing or official documentation |
| Integration support | Official documentation or testing |
| Performance impact | Controlled technical testing |
| Support quality | Direct test plus merchant patterns |
| Merchant complaints | Balanced review analysis |
| Best-fit merchant | Combined research findings |
| Final verdict | Full evaluation and scoring |

Unsupported claims must be removed or rewritten.

---

## **Step 24: Verify Public Claims Versus Blinto Findings**

The article must distinguish between:

* What the app company claims  
* What Blinto observed  
* What official documentation confirms  
* What merchants report  
* What Blinto did not test

### **Correct Language**

* The app claims to provide…  
* Official documentation states…  
* During Blinto’s test…  
* Merchant reviews commonly mention…  
* Blinto did not test…

### **Incorrect Language**

* The app definitely increases conversions.  
* All merchants receive fast support.  
* The integration works perfectly.  
* The app never affects performance.

---

## **Step 25: Review Merchant-Feedback Accuracy**

The reviewer should confirm that:

* Positive and negative patterns are included.  
* Feedback is recent where possible.  
* Old complaints are identified as older feedback.  
* Merchant opinions are not treated as facts.  
* Long customer reviews are not copied.  
* One unusual review is not presented as a trend.  
* The summary is balanced.

### **Example**

Acceptable:

**Recent reviews frequently mention helpful support, while some critical reviews report difficulty with advanced integrations.**

Not acceptable:

**The app has perfect support.**

---

# **13\. Technical Review**

## **Step 26: Validate Installation Claims**

The Shopify Expert should confirm:

* Installation steps are accurate.  
* Permission descriptions are correct.  
* External account requirements are explained.  
* Billing requirements are correct.  
* Developer assistance is not overstated or understated.  
* Setup difficulty reflects the actual test.  
* Theme changes are described accurately.

---

## **Step 27: Validate Feature Claims**

For every important feature, confirm:

* The feature currently exists.  
* The feature is available on the stated plan.  
* The feature works as described.  
* The feature has not been confused with another app.  
* Important restrictions are included.  
* Beta or experimental features are labeled.  
* Paid features are not described as free.

---

## **Step 28: Validate Integration Claims**

Confirm:

* The integration is officially supported.  
* The integration type is described correctly.  
* Required plans are mentioned.  
* External accounts are mentioned.  
* The integration was not described as tested unless it was tested.  
* Important limitations are included.

### **Integration Labels**

Use:

* Tested by Blinto  
* Confirmed through documentation  
* Listed by the app  
* Not tested

---

## **Step 29: Validate Shopify Plan Requirements**

Check whether the app or feature requires:

* Basic Shopify  
* Grow or Advanced Shopify  
* Shopify Plus  
* Checkout Extensibility  
* Shopify Flow  
* Customer accounts  
* B2B features  
* Specific markets or regions  
* External services

These requirements should be stated clearly where they affect the decision.

---

## **Step 30: Validate Theme and Storefront Claims**

Confirm:

* Theme compatibility observations are limited to the tested environment.  
* Storefront screenshots match the described result.  
* Mobile behavior is accurately described.  
* Custom theme requirements are disclosed.  
* Theme code changes are explained correctly.  
* App embed or theme extension terminology is accurate.

Avoid claiming compatibility with every theme.

---

## **Step 31: Validate Performance Claims**

Performance claims require extra caution.

### **Review:**

* Test environment  
* Before and after results  
* Test page  
* Testing method  
* Device or network conditions  
* Additional scripts  
* Console errors  
* Test limitations

### **Acceptable Wording**

**In the test environment, the app added additional storefront requests. The result may differ depending on the theme, app stack, and configuration.**

### **Unacceptable Wording**

**This app slows down every Shopify store.**

If the evidence is not strong enough, remove the performance claim.

---

## **Step 32: Validate Security and Privacy Claims**

The article should not make security guarantees unless supported by appropriate evidence.

Avoid:

* Completely secure  
* Fully compliant with every law  
* No privacy risk  
* Safe for every business

Where relevant, the article may mention:

* Requested permissions  
* Public privacy information  
* Data-access requirements  
* Merchant responsibilities

Serious security concerns must be escalated to the Project Lead.

---

## **Step 33: Validate Uninstall and Cleanup Claims**

If uninstall behavior is discussed, confirm:

* The app was actually uninstalled.  
* Remaining code or assets were checked.  
* The observation is limited to the test environment.  
* Cleanup requirements are explained accurately.

Avoid claiming that an app always leaves code based on one test.

---

# **14\. Pricing and Feature Verification**

## **Step 34: Recheck Pricing Before Approval**

Pricing should be rechecked close to the approval date.

### **Verify:**

* Free plan  
* Free trial  
* Starting price  
* Plan names  
* Feature restrictions  
* Usage-based pricing  
* Order limits  
* Revenue-based fees  
* Additional charges  
* Enterprise plans  
* Billing cycle

### **Pricing Requirements**

* Include a verification date.  
* Use official pricing sources.  
* Avoid copying outdated competitor pricing.  
* Clearly explain variable pricing.  
* Mark custom pricing as “contact sales” or similar.

If pricing changes after the draft is written, update the article and notes.

---

## **Step 35: Recheck App Availability and Rating**

Before final approval, confirm:

* The app is still available.  
* The developer name is unchanged.  
* The rating is current.  
* The review count is current where included.  
* The listing has not changed significantly.  
* The app has not been rebranded.  
* The app has not been acquired or discontinued.

Ratings and review counts should include a checked date when used in the article.

---

## **Step 36: Verify Feature Freshness**

Check whether:

* Features have been added.  
* Features have been removed.  
* Pricing tiers have changed.  
* Integrations have changed.  
* The dashboard has changed.  
* Screenshots are still current.  
* Documentation has changed.

Major product changes may require retesting.

---

# **15\. Category Review Quality Checks**

Category reviews require consistent evaluation across multiple apps.

## **Step 37: Review App Inclusion**

Confirm that every included app:

* Fits the category.  
* Solves the identified merchant problem.  
* Meets the evidence threshold.  
* Has a clear reason for inclusion.  
* Is still active.  
* Has enough information for a fair evaluation.

Remove apps included only to increase the list size.

---

## **Step 38: Review Recommendation Labels**

Labels such as the following must be justified:

* Best overall  
* Best for beginners  
* Best free option  
* Best for growing stores  
* Best for advanced merchants  
* Best for integrations  
* Best value

The article should explain the criteria behind each recommendation.

---

## **Step 39: Review Evaluation Consistency**

All apps should be evaluated using similar criteria.

Confirm:

* Pricing is compared consistently.  
* Features are compared consistently.  
* Testing levels are disclosed.  
* One app does not receive significantly more favorable coverage without reason.  
* Scores follow the same framework.  
* Weak evidence is not treated as equal to hands-on testing.

---

## **Step 40: Review Category Rankings**

If the apps are ranked, confirm that:

* Ranking criteria are defined.  
* The ranking follows the evidence.  
* Sponsorship did not affect the order.  
* The score and written verdict agree.  
* Apps with different use cases are not forced into an unfair ranking.

Grouping by use case may be better than a strict ranking.

---

# **16\. Comparison Article Quality Checks**

## **Step 41: Review Comparison Fairness**

Confirm that both apps are evaluated using:

* The same merchant scenario  
* Similar pricing levels  
* The same feature criteria  
* The same usability criteria  
* The same integration criteria  
* The same scoring system  
* Similar depth

Avoid giving one app more positive detail simply because the team has a relationship with its founder.

---

## **Step 42: Review Winner Statements**

Every winner statement should explain:

* Which criterion was used  
* Why the app performed better  
* Which merchant benefits  
* Whether the advantage is significant

### **Weak Statement**

**Judge.me wins.**

### **Better Statement**

**Judge.me is the stronger option for budget-conscious merchants because its free plan includes the core review-collection features that many small stores need.**

---

## **Step 43: Review the Overall Verdict**

The comparison should explain:

* When App A is better  
* When App B is better  
* Which merchants should choose each  
* Whether there is a clear overall winner  
* What limitations affect the choice

Do not force an overall winner when the apps suit different merchants.

---

# **17\. Internal Linking Review**

## **Step 44: Review Internal-Link Relevance**

Confirm that internal links:

* Are contextually relevant.  
* Help the reader continue their journey.  
* Support the content cluster.  
* Use descriptive anchor text.  
* Do not interrupt readability.  
* Do not overuse service links.

### **Recommended Link Types**

* Parent category article  
* Supporting app review  
* Comparison article  
* Educational guide  
* Relevant Shopify service page  
* Relevant case study

---

## **Step 45: Review Anchor Text**

Anchor text should:

* Describe the destination.  
* Sound natural.  
* Avoid generic wording such as “click here.”  
* Avoid exact-match repetition.  
* Match the page topic.

### **Example**

Good:

**Compare the best Shopify product review apps.**

Weak:

**Click here to learn more.**

---

## **Step 46: Check Planned Links**

When a destination page is not yet published:

* Keep a clear placeholder.  
* Add it to the publishing checklist.  
* Record the planned URL.  
* Confirm whether the link should be added later.

Broken or invented URLs should not appear in the final draft.

* Is the CTA helpful?  
* Is the wording clear?  
* Does the CTA avoid aggressive sales language?  
* Does it preserve editorial independence?

---

## **Step 48: Review CTA Placement**

Recommended placements:

* One contextual CTA where strongly relevant  
* One primary CTA after the final verdict

Avoid:

* Multiple repetitive CTAs  
* CTA banners after every section  
* Sales language before the reader receives value  
* CTAs unrelated to the article

---

## **Step 49: Review CTA Claims**

Do not claim that Blinto can:

* Guarantee conversions  
* Guarantee app performance  
* Remove all technical risk  
* Guarantee higher sales  
* Guarantee a specific result

Use realistic service language.

# **21\. Revision Management**

## **Step 57: Classify Review Comments**

Review comments should be categorized.

| Category | Meaning |
| ----- | ----- |
| Editorial | Clarity, tone, structure, repetition, grammar |
| SEO | Search intent, keyword, heading, metadata, linking |
| Research | Missing or incorrectly used evidence |
| Technical | Incorrect Shopify or app information |
| Pricing | Outdated or unclear pricing |
| Trust | Disclosure, testing level, independence |
| Strategic | Content angle, verdict, audience, CTA |
| Visual | Missing screenshots, tables, or design requirements |

This helps the writer understand what must be changed.

---

## **Step 58: Set Revision Priority**

| Priority | Meaning |
| ----- | ----- |
| Critical | Misleading, false, unsupported, risky, or legally sensitive |
| High | Search intent, technical accuracy, pricing, verdict, or structural issue |
| Medium | Missing context, weak explanation, incomplete evidence |
| Low | Style, wording, formatting, or minor grammar |

Critical and high-priority issues must be resolved before approval.

---

## **Step 59: Assign Revisions**

The Project Manager or Content Lead should:

* Assign comments to the writer.  
* Set a revision deadline.  
* Identify comments requiring researcher input.  
* Identify comments requiring developer input.  
* Separate required changes from optional suggestions.  
* Avoid conflicting reviewer comments.

When reviewers disagree, the Content Lead or Project Lead makes the final decision.

---

## **Step 60: Complete Revisions**

The writer must:

* Resolve every required comment.  
* Respond to comments that need explanation.  
* Update pricing dates where required.  
* Mark technical corrections.  
* Update visual placeholders.  
* Update internal links.  
* Confirm disclosure changes.  
* Update writer notes.

The writer should not delete unresolved comments without approval.

---

## **Step 61: Conduct Revision Review**

The responsible reviewer confirms:

* Required corrections were made.  
* Changes did not create new errors.  
* Technical corrections are accurate.  
* SEO corrections remain natural.  
* The final verdict is consistent.  
* Evidence still supports the content.  
* Pricing remains current.  
* All critical and high-priority comments are resolved.

If major issues remain, the task returns to **Revision Required**.

# **26\. Quality Standards**

Content is considered ready for approval when:

* It answers the main search query clearly.  
* The intended merchant can make a better decision.  
* The article provides original value.  
* Testing claims are accurate.  
* Pricing and features are current.  
* Technical statements are supported.  
* Recommendations are contextual.  
* Limitations are clearly visible.  
* Comparisons are fair.  
* Keywords are natural.  
* Internal links support the content cluster.  
* The CTA is relevant.  
* Disclosures are complete.  
* The draft is clean and publishable.

---

# **27\. Common Mistakes to Avoid**

Do not:

* Review the article without checking the brief.  
* Approve content that does not satisfy search intent.  
* Focus only on grammar.  
* Optimize keywords before fixing structural problems.  
* Accept generic feature summaries.  
* Allow unsupported claims.  
* Ignore limitations.  
* Use outdated pricing.  
* Approve technical claims without validation.  
* Treat app marketing statements as facts.  
* Hide partial testing.  
* Allow founder influence over the verdict.  
* Force internal links.  
* Add aggressive CTAs.  
* Approve inconsistent comparison criteria.  
* Rank apps without explaining the methodology.  
* Approve unresolved critical comments.  
* Move the content to design before final approval.  
* Use the designer or publisher as the final fact-checker.

---

# **28\. Escalation Rules**

Escalate to the Project Lead when:

* The article contains fabricated testing.  
* The research does not support the verdict.  
* Pricing or features change significantly.  
* The app is removed from the Shopify App Store.  
* Serious privacy or security concerns are identified.  
* A founder requests editorial control.  
* A founder disputes factual content.  
* A partnership creates an independence concern.  
* The article creates legal or reputational risk.  
* Reviewers disagree about a critical claim.  
* The primary keyword no longer matches the article.  
* The content duplicates an existing Blinto page.  
* The app requires technical retesting.  
* The article cannot provide meaningful unique value.

The Project Lead may require:

* Additional research  
* Technical retesting  
* A revised verdict  
* A format change  
* Legal or leadership review  
* Delayed publication  
* App replacement  
* Content cancellation
