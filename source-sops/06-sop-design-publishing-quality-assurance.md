# **1\. Purpose**

The purpose of this SOP is to define how Blinto prepares approved Shopify app review content for publication, creates and processes supporting visuals, implements the article in the content management system, completes final quality assurance, publishes the content, and verifies that the live page works correctly.

This process ensures that every Shopify app review:

* Maintains the accuracy and structure of the approved content.  
* Uses clear, useful, and consistent visual elements.  
* Provides a strong reading experience on desktop and mobile.  
* Includes properly formatted tables, screenshots, links, metadata, and calls to action.  
* Meets Blinto’s SEO, accessibility, technical, and editorial standards.  
* Is crawlable and indexable.  
* Uses appropriate canonical and structured-data settings.  
* Displays correct author, reviewer, testing, and update information.  
* Contains no broken links, missing images, formatting problems, or publishing errors.  
* Is ready for promotion, performance tracking, and future maintenance.

No Shopify app review should be published without completing this SOP.

---

# **2\. SOP Objective**

This SOP helps Blinto:

* Maintain a consistent design system across all Shopify app review content.  
* Turn the approved draft into a polished live article.  
* Make complex app information easier to understand.  
* Protect the accuracy of the approved article during CMS implementation.  
* Improve readability and user experience.  
* Implement on-page SEO requirements correctly.  
* Prevent broken links, incorrect metadata, and indexing problems.  
* Apply appropriate structured data.  
* Confirm that disclosures and trust signals are visible.

* Create a clear publication and post-publication verification process.  
* Prepare the article for promotion, measurement, and maintenance.

# **10\. Featured Image Creation**

## **Step 3: Create the Featured Image**

The featured image should make the content topic immediately understandable.

### **Featured Image Requirements**

The image should:

* Follow Blinto’s visual identity.  
* Clearly reflect the app, category, or comparison.  
* Remain readable at thumbnail size.  
* Avoid overcrowding.  
* Avoid unverified ratings or claims.  
* Avoid copyrighted promotional material that Blinto cannot use.  
* Use approved app logos only where permitted.  
* Work across blog cards, social previews, and the article page.  
* Be consistent with other content in the project.

### **Recommended Featured Image Approaches**

#### **Single App Review**

* App name  
* Review label  
* App category  
* Simple Shopify-related visual  
* App dashboard or interface-inspired element where permitted

#### **Comparison Article**

* App A versus App B  
* Clear side-by-side visual  
* Main comparison context  
* No unsupported “winner” label before the article verdict

#### **Category Review**

* Category title  
* Multiple app or workflow elements  
* Merchant problem or use-case theme  
* Clear “best apps” context

### **Featured Image Text**

Keep image text short.

Examples:

* Judge.me Review  
* Judge.me vs Loox  
* Best Shopify Review Apps  
* Top Shopify Dropshipping Apps

Do not place the full SEO title on the image.

---

## **Step 4: Review Logo and Brand Usage**

Before using third-party logos:

* Confirm that the logo is publicly available for standard identification use.  
* Do not alter the logo in a misleading way.  
* Do not imply endorsement or partnership.  
* Do not use badges such as “official partner” unless Blinto has that status.  
* Do not place one app’s logo more prominently because of a commercial relationship.  
* Follow any documented brand-usage requirements.

Where logo use is unclear, use neutral text or interface-based visual elements.

# **11\. Screenshot Preparation**

## **Step 5: Select Screenshots**

Use screenshots that support important findings.

### **Possible Screenshot Types**

* App Store listing  
* Installation screen  
* Permission screen  
* Onboarding checklist  
* Main dashboard  
* Feature configuration  
* Pricing interface  
* Storefront output  
* Mobile output  
* Integration settings  
* Reporting dashboard  
* Support interface  
* Error or limitation  
* Uninstall process

### **Screenshot Selection Rules**

Choose screenshots that:

* Demonstrate an important feature.  
* Support a statement in the article.  
* Help the reader understand setup or usability.  
* Show an original Blinto test where available.  
* Reflect the current app interface.  
* Can be understood with a short caption.

Do not include screenshots only to make the article appear longer.

---

## **Step 6: Protect Private and Sensitive Information**

Before publishing a screenshot, remove or hide:

* Email addresses  
* Passwords  
* API keys  
* Access tokens  
* Store URLs where private  
* Customer names  
* Customer addresses  
* Order details  
* Payment information  
* Private support messages  
* Internal account identifiers  
* Client information  
* Confidential test data

Use test data wherever possible.

### **Privacy Review**

Every screenshot must be marked:

* Approved for publication  
* Requires editing  
* Internal use only  
* Rejected

Internal evidence should not automatically be treated as publishable.

---

## **Step 7: Prepare Screenshot Presentation**

Screenshots should be:

* Cropped to the relevant area.  
* Clear and readable.  
* Free from unnecessary browser clutter.  
* Consistent in visual treatment.  
* Large enough for important interface text.  
* Usable on mobile screens.  
* Supported by a caption where needed.

Do not crop away information necessary to understand the screenshot.

### **Annotation Rules**

Annotations may be used to:

* Highlight a setting  
* Show a workflow step  
* Identify a feature  
* Explain an interface element

Annotations must not:

* Change the meaning of the interface.  
* Hide an app limitation.  
* Create a false result.  
* Suggest functionality that was not present.

# **13\. Image Optimization**

## **Step 12: Name Image Files**

Use descriptive filenames.

### **File-Naming Format**

**\[primary-subject\]-\[feature-or-context\].\[filetype\]**

Examples:

* `judgeme-review-dashboard.webp`  
* `judgeme-vs-loox-comparison.webp`  
* `best-shopify-review-apps.webp`  
* `loox-onboarding-screen.webp`

### **File-Naming Rules**

* Use lowercase letters.  
* Use hyphens.  
* Avoid unnecessary numbers.  
* Avoid spaces.  
* Avoid generic names such as `image1.png`.  
* Avoid keyword stuffing.  
* Use the correct app name.

---

## **Step 13: Compress and Format Images**

Before upload:

* Resize images to appropriate display dimensions.  
* Compress files without making interface text unreadable.  
* Use a suitable modern image format where supported.  
* Keep transparent images only when transparency is necessary.  
* Avoid uploading unnecessarily large source files.  
* Test image quality on both desktop and mobile.

### **Image Quality Rule**

Compression should not make screenshots difficult to read.

Interface screenshots may require higher clarity than decorative graphics.

---

## **Step 14: Add Alt Text**

Alt text should describe the image’s purpose and visible content.

### **Good Alt Text**

* Judge.me onboarding checklist showing product review setup steps  
* Comparison of Judge.me and Loox Shopify app pricing plans  
* Shopify development store displaying a Loox photo review widget  
* Best Shopify review apps comparison table

### **Alt-Text Rules**

* Describe the image accurately.  
* Mention the app or feature where relevant.  
* Keep it concise.  
* Do not repeat surrounding text unnecessarily.  
* Do not use “image of” unless required for clarity.  
* Do not add keywords unrelated to the image.  
* Use empty alt text for purely decorative images where appropriate.

---

## **Step 15: Add Captions**

Use captions when they:

* Explain what the screenshot shows.  
* Clarify the testing environment.  
* Identify a limitation.  
* Provide the screenshot date.  
* Distinguish a tested feature from an official product image.

### **Example**

**Blinto tested Judge.me’s initial review-widget setup using a Shopify development store.**

Captions should not make unsupported claims.

## **Step 17: Set the URL Slug**

The slug should:

* Be concise.  
* Include the main topic.  
* Use lowercase letters and hyphens.  
* Avoid unnecessary dates unless the strategy requires them.  
* Avoid stop words where they add no value.  
* Avoid changing an existing indexed URL without a redirect plan.

### **Examples**

* `/shopify-apps/judgeme-review/`  
* `/shopify-apps/judgeme-vs-loox/`  
* `/shopify-apps/best-review-apps/`  
* `/shopify-apps/best-dropshipping-apps/`

### **Duplicate URL Check**

Before confirming the slug:

* Search the CMS.  
* Check the sitemap.  
* Search the website.  
* Confirm the URL is not already used.  
* Confirm no unpublished draft uses the same slug.

# **19\. Structured Data and Technical SEO**

## **Step 35: Select Appropriate Structured Data**

The SEO Strategist should select markup that accurately reflects visible page content and current search-engine guidance.

Possible markup may include:

* Article or BlogPosting  
* BreadcrumbList  
* Person  
* Organization  
* FAQ markup only when appropriate and supported  
* Other applicable types approved by the SEO Lead

### **Structured-Data Rules**

* Mark up only content visible on the page.  
* Do not add false ratings.  
* Do not create unsupported review or aggregate-rating markup.  
* Do not mark Blinto’s own editorial score as third-party user reviews.  
* Do not add FAQ markup to content that does not contain visible FAQs.  
* Use consistent author, publisher, date, image, and canonical information.  
* Validate the final markup before publication.  
* Follow current search-engine and Schema.org guidance.

Structured data may help search engines understand a page, but it must not be treated as a guarantee of enhanced search appearance.

# **33\. Common Mistakes to Avoid**

Do not:

* Start design before the article is approved.  
* Use an older content version.  
* Change the verdict during CMS upload.  
* Hide important limitations in the design.  
* Use low-quality or irrelevant stock images.  
* Publish screenshots containing private data.  
* Use outdated app screenshots.  
* Upload oversized images.  
* Use generic image filenames.  
* Add keyword-stuffed alt text.  
* Create unreadable mobile tables.  
* Add unverified ratings to graphics.  
* Add unsupported review schema.  
* Use the wrong canonical.  
* Publish with noindex enabled.  
* Link to staging pages.  
* Publish broken internal links.  
* Add an unrelated CTA.  
* Forget testing or affiliate disclosures.  
* Assign an incorrect author.  
* Update the publication date without meaningful changes.  
* Skip mobile QA.  
* Assume the preview and live page will behave identically.  
* Publish without checking analytics.  
* Complete the task before live-page verification.
